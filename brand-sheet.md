# ByTheWey Brand Sheet

## Brand summary
ByTheWey is a hyperlocal community PWA for Weybridge (Elmbridge, Surrey). It helps residents discover genuinely good local places, keep up with community events, and connect with neighbours through a moderated forum. The product should feel calm, trusted, and curated - like a riverside local paper rebuilt for mobile.

## Audience
- Families (weekend plans, kids activities, reliable "what's on")
- Young couples (date nights, coffee spots, new openings, "hidden gems")
- Older couples (daytime activities, dependable recommendations, community updates)
- Long-term residents (local pride, community conversation, practical info)

## Brand metaphor
Riverside local paper, rebuilt as a PWA.

## Brand traits
- Calm
- Trustworthy
- Curated
- Neighbourly

## Tone of voice rules
- Keep it short, warm, and practical.
- Be specific: say "Weybridge" (or the named area) rather than "local".
- Prefer helpful over clever. No hype, no salesy language.
- Use "Edited pick" language for curated content and "Community" language for user posts.
- Write like a good local editor: confident, polite, and grounded.
- Defaults: plain English, minimal punctuation, avoid jargon.

## Visual identity direction

### Logo and icon
We need an icon that reads clearly at small sizes and feels "Weybridge" without looking touristy.

Primary icon concept (Option 1):
- A simplified bridge arch over a meandering river line that forms a subtle "W".

Backup concepts:
- Option 2: A river bend that creates a "W" in negative space inside a rounded square icon.
- Option 3: A minimal bridge silhouette where the underside arch suggests a "W" (single-colour mark).

Icon principles:
- Simple silhouette, no tiny details.
- Works in monochrome and as a maskable PWA icon.
- Calm and premium, not "startup loud".

### Background texture / pattern
Use a subtle, low-contrast texture only in heroes and empty states:
- Option A: thin "River Wey" linework (gentle curves).
- Option B: faint map contour lines (topographic feel).

Rules:
- Must not reduce text readability.
- Keep it understated and consistent.

## Colour direction (no final hex codes yet)
- Primary: Wey Teal (used for navigation state, key actions, and headers)
- Accent: River Mist (used for highlights, chips, and subtle emphasis)
- Neutrals: warm off-white surfaces, soft slate text (avoid harsh black on white)
- Category colours: subtle tints that follow a consistent saturation rule (calm, not candy)

Colour rules:
- The UI should feel quiet and readable.
- Category colours should never compete with content.
- Use colour to guide, not decorate.

## Typography direction (no final font decision yet)
- Headings: a readable editorial serif (adds "curated local paper" character)
- Body/UI: a modern sans (keeps the app clean and fast)

Typography rules:
- Prioritise legibility on mobile.
- Avoid thin weights for body text.
- Keep hierarchy obvious: headings, summaries, metadata, tags.

## UI principles
- Legibility first: strong contrast, comfortable line height, generous spacing.
- Tap targets: assume one-handed use, make chips and buttons easy to hit.
- Trust cues everywhere:
  - "Edited pick" for guide highlights
  - "Verified details" and "Last checked" where relevant
  - "Report issue" for wrong hours or info
- Quiet premium: restrained colour use, consistent cards, minimal visual noise.
- Community safety: moderation should feel present but not heavy-handed.

## Implementation definition of done
When we start applying this brand system, we are done when:
- A single token system drives colour, radius, shadow, and typography.
- Home, Guide, Events, and Forum screens look like one product family.
- The icon works as a maskable PWA icon and favicon.
- Hero areas and empty states use the same subtle background texture rules.
- Copy feels consistent: short, local, calm, and trustworthy.
- Category colours are consistent and do not reduce readability.
